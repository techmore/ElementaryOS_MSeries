[Titus]
title: About the Letter
insight: Paul wrote this short letter to Titus, a Greek coworker he trusted with hard assignments. Titus was not circumcised (Galatians 2:3), went with Paul to Jerusalem (Galatians 2:1), and carried the delicate work of reconciliation in Corinth (2 Corinthians 7:6–7; 8:16–23). ¶ Paul had left Titus on the island of Crete to “set in order the things that were lacking” and appoint elders in every city (1:5). Acts does not record this mission, so many date the letter to the mid-60s AD, after Paul’s first imprisonment in Rome. With 1 and 2 Timothy it is one of the Pastoral Letters. ¶ The thread through all three chapters is that true teaching and a good life belong together. “Good works” appears six times (1:16; 2:7, 14; 3:1, 8, 14), and “Savior” is used of God (1:3; 2:10; 3:4) and of Jesus (1:4; 2:13; 3:6). The grace that saves is also the grace that trains (2:11–12). ¶ Outline • 1:1–4 A greeting full of the gospel • 1:5–9 Leaders who hold to the word • 1:10–16 Truth against empty talk • 2:1–10 Sound teaching lived out at home • 2:11–15 Grace that trains us • 3:1–7 Kindness that saved us • 3:8–11 Devoted to good works • 3:12–15 Partners in the mission
reflect: Where do what you believe and how you live most need to meet this week?

[Titus 1:1-4]
title: A Greeting Full of the Gospel
insight: Paul’s greeting is a compressed sermon. He serves the faith of God’s people and a “knowledge of the truth which is according to godliness”: truth that changes how people live, not just what they know. That hope rests on a God “who can’t lie” and who promised eternal life before time began (1:2). Paul calls Titus “my true child according to a common faith”, a family bond formed by shared faith rather than shared background.
reflect: How does knowing that God cannot lie steady the hopes you are holding right now?

[Titus 1:5-9]
title: Leaders Who Hold to the Word
insight: Titus’s first task is to appoint elders in every city. The list is mostly about character, not talent: blameless, faithful at home, not quick-tempered, not greedy, hospitable, self-controlled. An overseer is “God’s steward” (1:7), managing what belongs to someone else. The one skill named is holding firmly to the faithful word, so that he can encourage with sound teaching and answer those who contradict it (1:9).
reflect: Which quality in this list would you most like God to grow in you, whether or not you lead?

[Titus 1:10-16]
title: Truth Against Empty Talk
insight: Some teachers in Crete were upsetting whole households for money (1:11). Paul quotes a Cretan prophet, traditionally identified as the poet Epimenides, to show that even Cretans knew the danger of empty words. Titus is to correct them sharply, with the goal that they be “sound in the faith” (1:13): restoration, not just winning an argument. The final test is practical: people can profess to know God “but by their deeds they deny him” (1:16).
reflect: Where might your words about God and your actions be telling different stories?

[Titus 2:1-5]
title: Sound Teaching at Home
insight: “Sound doctrine” (2:1) is healthy teaching, and Paul shows what it looks like in ordinary households in Crete. Older men and older women are called to steady, self-controlled lives, and older women are to be “teachers of that which is good”, passing faith on to the next generation. The reason given is outward-facing: “that God’s word may not be blasphemed” (2:5). Everyday faithfulness at home either commends the gospel to neighbors or discredits it.
reflect: Who has modeled faith for you, and who could you encourage the way they encouraged you?

[Titus 2:6-10]
title: Lives That Adorn the Teaching
insight: Paul turns to younger men and to Titus himself: “show yourself an example of good works” (2:7). Teaching needs integrity and speech that can’t be fairly condemned. Paul then speaks to believers who were household servants within the social structures of the first-century world. The aim is that they “adorn the doctrine of God, our Savior, in all things” (2:10). To adorn is to make beautiful: faithful lives make the gospel attractive.
reflect: In your work or daily routine, what would it look like to make the gospel beautiful to the people around you?

[Titus 2:11-15]
title: Grace That Trains Us
insight: This is the heart of the letter. God’s grace “has appeared, bringing salvation” (2:11), and the same grace is a teacher, training us to say no to ungodliness and to live “soberly, righteously, and godly in this present age” (2:12). We live between two appearings: grace has appeared, and we wait for “the blessed hope and appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ” (2:13), a phrase many readers see as calling Jesus God. He gave himself to redeem us and to form a people “zealous for good works” (2:14).
reflect: Grace saves and grace trains. Where is God’s grace training you right now?

[Titus 3:1-7]
title: Kindness That Saved Us
insight: Christians in Crete were to be good citizens: ready for every good work, gentle, and showing humility toward everyone (3:1–2). Paul grounds that gentleness in memory: “we were also once foolish” (3:3). Then comes one of the New Testament’s richest summaries of salvation. When God’s kindness and love appeared, he saved us, not by our own righteous works but by his mercy, through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit, poured out through Jesus Christ. Justified by grace, we become heirs with the hope of eternal life (3:4–7). Father, Son and Spirit are all at work in these few verses.
reflect: How does remembering who you were before God’s kindness change how you treat difficult people?

[Titus 3:8-11]
title: Devoted to Good Works
insight: “This saying is faithful” (3:8) points back to the summary of salvation in 3:4–7, and Paul wants Titus to stress it so that believers “may be careful to maintain good works”. Good works flow from grace; they don’t earn it. By contrast, foolish arguments about genealogies and the law are “unprofitable and vain” (3:9). A person who keeps stirring up division is to be warned twice, then avoided (3:10), which protects the church’s unity without endless quarrels.
reflect: Which conversations in your life build people up, and which only stir up division?

[Titus 3:12-15]
title: Partners in the Mission
insight: The letter closes with travel plans and names. Artemas or Tychicus will relieve Titus so he can meet Paul at Nicopolis, probably the city in western Greece, for the winter. Tychicus also appears in Ephesians 6:21 and Colossians 4:7, and Apollos is the gifted teacher of Acts 18. Zenas the lawyer is known only from this verse. Even practical hospitality, helping traveling workers “that nothing may be lacking for them” (3:13), is part of learning “to maintain good works” (3:14). The letter ends as it began: “Grace be with you all.”
reflect: Who is serving God near you that you could help on their way this week?
